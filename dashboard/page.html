What price sensitivity means

Elasticity says how many units we lose when the price goes up. It is measured from Fresmart's own sales history.

ElasticityA 10% price rise losesReads as
−0.5about 5% of unitsLow sensitivity
−1.0about 10% of unitsMedium
−2.0about 20% of unitsHigh sensitivity

Evidence says how sure we are: High means many past price changes to learn from; Low means few, so the estimate leans on similar articles.

How to read this

  1. GP and revenue here are each SKU's own. Sales recaptured by substitute articles are left out because several SKUs move at once, so GP differs slightly and revenue more from the Overview.
  2. ML recommended changes are rounded to the nearest whole % to match the simulation grid.
  3. Warnings: outside the price range the data has tested, over the group's volume limit (A 10%, B–D 15%), or a gross profit loss.
  4. Plans are kept while the page is open and are not shared with others. The approved list is on the Price list tab.

Monitoring rules

  1. Each week, compare actual chain units per SKU with the expected line.
  2. Below the band two weeks running, with stock on the shelf: review the price. Three weeks: roll back.
  3. Above the band: check stock and competitor moves. No price action.
  4. Unit cost moves more than 5%, or a competitor moves on a staple: re-run the price for that SKU.
  5. The band covers the cautious price response and normal forecast error (about ±22% per SKU-month), so it catches large misses only. The first reliable reading is after about four weeks.

How the prices were set

  1. Extraction from Azure SQL: sales, prices, costs, stock
  2. Cleaning: units, outliers, clearance weeks
  3. Exploratory analysis
  4. Integration with macro data (FX, CPI, Brent, GDP)
  5. Feature engineering: store, week, SKU
  6. Feature selection: RFE, Lasso, SelectKBest
  7. PCA on macro inputs
  8. Statistical tests on price effects
  9. Elasticity: linear, multiple, log-log, shrunk to group evidence
  10. Demand models: Ridge, RF, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost
  11. Training and rolling evaluation
  12. SHAP explanations
  13. Demand simulation at each price
  14. Revenue and gross profit
  15. Optimisation with guardrails, substitutes priced jointly
  16. Recommendations: shelf prices, waves, test design
  17. Business rule validation
  18. This dashboard
